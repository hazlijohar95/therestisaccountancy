> The schedule sits behind your engagement letter. The letter carries your terms of business. The schedule says what this client gets. Keep the two apart, so a change in scope is a new schedule, not a new letter.
>
> Write the scope as work you can point to. "Monthly bookkeeping" invites an argument. "Bank and card transactions coded each month, up to 150 lines" does not.
>
> A schedule is not a substitute for terms of business. Your engagement letter's terms, and anything here about responsibility or liability, should be your own and checked by an adviser.

## Schedule of services

| Tax return | The return and its computation for the year | Yearly | [Number] weeks before it is due |
| [Another service] | [What it covers] | [How often] | [When] |

### 2. What we will not do, unless we agree it in writing

- Audit or assurance work of any kind
- Bookkeeping above [Number] lines in a month
- Catching up on records for any period before [Date]
- Answering an enquiry or review by an authority
- Advice on a transaction outside the ordinary run of the business, such as a sale, a restructure or a property purchase
- [Anything else this client is likely to ask for]

If you ask for any of this, we will quote for it first and start only once you agree.

### 3. What we need from you

- Bank and card statements, or access to them, by the [Number]th of each month
- Sales and purchase invoices, receipts and contracts as they happen, through [Shared folder or portal]
- Answers to our questions within [Number] working days
- Word of anything new: a bank account, a line of business, staff, a loan, an asset bought or sold
- Complete and accurate information. Our work relies on it, and we do not audit it

Either of us can ask to change it. A change takes effect when we have both signed a new schedule. Until then, this one applies.
