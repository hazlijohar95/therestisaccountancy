> Do it with your client list open. The answers are in the clients you already have, not in a market report.
>
> Expect the first sentence you write to be too broad. "Small businesses" is not a position. "Family-run restaurants in the Klang Valley that want to open a second outlet" is.

What do at least three of them share? [Industry, size, stage, kind of owner or problem]

### 2. The problem you solve

- The problem they had before they came to you: [In their words]
- What it was costing them: [Time, money, sleep, a missed chance]
- What changed afterwards: [In their words]

### 3. What you know that others don't

- An industry you understand from the inside: [Industry]
- Work you have done many times over: [Work]
- Something from your own life or career that a client in that industry would value: [Experience]

### 4. The sentence

Write it three times, narrower each time.

> I help [Who, as exactly as you can] who [Have this problem] to [Get this result].

1. [First version]
2. [Narrower]
3. [Narrower still]

### 5. Test it

- [ ] Read the third version to someone outside accounting. Can they repeat it back?
- [ ] Can they name someone who fits it? [Who they named]
- [ ] Would three of your five best clients recognise themselves in it?
- [ ] Are there enough clients like this for a firm your size? [Your estimate, and how you reached it]

### 6. What you will turn away

A position is also what you stop doing. The work you will refer elsewhere from now on: [The work, and who you will refer it to]
