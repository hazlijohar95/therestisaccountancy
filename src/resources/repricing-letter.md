title: Repricing letter to an existing client
programme: pricing

summary: A letter that tells a client their fee is changing, why, and from when, without apologising for it.
---

> Send it to the clients your margin worksheet put below your floor. Speak to the client first, by phone or in person, so the letter confirms a conversation rather than starts one.
>
> Read your engagement letter before you write. If it says how fees change, or how much notice you give, follow it. If it says nothing, give at least two months, and three for a large rise.
>
> Offer no discount. If the client pushes back, offer less work for less money. The price of the work stays the same.

[Date]

Dear [Client's first name],

**Your fee from [Date the new fee starts]**

Thank you for the last [Number] years. It has been good to work with [Business name] as it has grown.

I review every client's fee once a year, against the work it takes. Your work has grown since we last agreed yours. [One sentence on what has grown, such as: you now have six staff on payroll and twice the transactions you had when we started.]

From [Date the new fee starts], your fee will be RM [New fee] a month, up from RM [Current fee]. It covers:

- [Service, as it is now]
- [Service, as it is now]
- [Service, as it is now]

Nothing else changes. You keep me as your accountant, and the same [Replies, reminders and meetings] you have now.

If you would rather keep your fee where it is, I can reduce the work to fit it. I would suggest [The smaller package, or the work that would stop]. Let me know by [A date a month before the change] which you prefer, and I will send a new schedule of services for you to sign.

If you have any questions, call me on [Phone number] or reply to this letter.

Yours sincerely,

[Your name]\
[Your firm's name]
