Most fees start from a competitor's price list. This one starts from your own costs. The floor rate is the least an hour of your billed time can earn and still pay you what you set out to earn.

## The three numbers

1. **Revenue to cover.** The income you want this year, plus everything it costs to run the practice: rent, software, insurance, subscriptions and any help you pay for.
2. **Billable hours.** The hours you work in a week, times the weeks you actually work, less the share of that time nobody pays for. Admin, sales, chasing invoices and learning all count as unpaid.
3. **The floor.** Revenue to cover, divided by billable hours, rounded up to the next ringgit. A floor is never rounded down.

## A worked example

Say you want RM 120,000 this year and the practice costs RM 30,000 to run. You work 40 hours a week, take 6 weeks off, and 40% of your time is unpaid.

- Revenue to cover is RM 150,000.
- You work 46 weeks. 40 hours times 46 weeks is 1,840 hours. Only 60% of those are billed, so 1,104 hours.
- RM 150,000 divided by 1,104 hours is RM 135.87. The floor is RM 136 an hour.

A client who takes four hours a month is therefore worth at least RM 544 a month to you. Below that, the client costs you money.

## What the floor is not

The floor is not your price. It is the number your price must never fall under. Packages, the next module, sit above it, priced on the value of the work to the client.

## Do this now

Open the [rate tool](/tools/rate) and put in your own numbers. Write down your floor. Then list your five largest clients and the hours each takes a month. Mark every one paying less than your floor times their hours. Those are the first clients to reprice.
